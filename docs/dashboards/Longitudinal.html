Longitudinal

The analytical procedure:

  • To study participants’ psychological functioning over time, we combined data from nine survey waves collected between 2020 and 2025 (5 annual, 4 COVID-specific):

  1. COVID wave I (N = 10 331): March - April 2020

  2. COVID wave II (N = 17 157): September - November 2020

  3. End of 2020 (N = 30 158): December 2020

  4. COVID wave III (N = 27 811): March - May 2021

  5. End of 2021 (N = 21 112): December 2021

  6. Post-COVID (N = 24 295): March-April 2022

  7. End of 2022 (N = 16 836): October 2022

  8. End of 2024 (N = 15 065): December 2024

  9. End of 2025 (N = 10 296): December 2025

  • Only respondents who participated in a sufficient number of waves were included ((#waves/2)+1), ensuring that the trends reflect genuine longitudinal change rather than one-off responses.

  • To correct for over- or under-representation of specific sociodemographic groups, responses were weighted by age group, gender, and region, separately for each wave.

  • For data quality and reliability, sociodemographic subgroups with fewer than 40 participants within a wave were excluded from analyses. Weighted mean scores were then calculated for each item at each wave, and vizualised as longitudinal trends. Alongside this, we checked whether the sociodemographic composition of the sample differed across waves using descriptive statistics and chi-square tests (see tab ‘Sociodemographics’).
